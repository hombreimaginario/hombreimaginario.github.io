Karnov has had less of my time than Tiger Road lately, but it keeps moving. The continue screen now asks you to PRESS START BUTTON TO CONTINUE, and A, B or C speed up the countdown. On a real console some dots showed up on screen whenever colours changed, because they were written while the picture was being drawn. Now they're only written during the vertical blank and the dots are gone. Most of the work, though, has gone into the two things that give away any port to weaker hardware: slowdown and flicker.

The arcade game shares nothing with anyone. On the Mega Drive it shares the 68000 with my glue code, which converts its sprites, colours and background every frame. At the end of October a fixed test run lost 283 frames out of 1,800. The MAME trace showed where they went. The game cleared its sprite list one long word at a time and loaded each of its 40 objects into memory before checking whether it was even active. I rewrote a handful of those routines in assembly, and 16×32 sprites are now a single Mega Drive sprite instead of two. The same run now loses around 15 frames.

Colours were the next fight. The Mega Drive has four palettes of 16 colours, and the first stage alone uses five background palettes and eight for sprites. With a fixed plan per stage, whatever didn't fit came out in someone else's colours. I tried sharing the colours out on the fly, according to what's on screen. It looked better, but frames lost went from 8 to 25 and an enemy could change colour while you were looking at it. My rule is that an enemy can have a different colour from the arcade, but it can't change in front of you. So each enemy palette now has a fixed place in each stage, and the enemy tiles seen in MAME are stored in the ROM already converted. The CPU converts four times less, and we're back to 11 lost frames. Along the way the enemies came out full of holes for a while: the buffer the DMA copied them from sat at an odd address and everything arrived shifted by one byte.

The underwater stage, the fifth, is the worst. Schools of fish put up to 15 objects on screen at once. With 15 active the Mega Drive loses 28% of the frames, and with 13 or fewer, about 1%. Half of that time is my sprite code. The other half is the game itself, mostly the check of every enemy against Karnov's fireballs. Unrolling that routine took the stage from about 90 lost frames to 70 out of 3,559. Flicker is a different limit. In this mode the Mega Drive draws 64 sprites at most, and 16 per line. In stages 6 to 9 the arcade goes over 64 in 6 to 9% of the frames, up to 87 in the ninth, and over 16 per line about as often. On the Mega Drive whatever doesn't fit isn't drawn.

I hoped the difficulty setting would help, but in the arcade it only changes how tough and how aggressive the enemies are, never how many appear. So it will probably be like Tiger Road, where only one kite man at a time made level 3 playable: fewer fish at once in the fifth stage, and a look at the later stages to see whether the missing sprites are noticeable or not.
